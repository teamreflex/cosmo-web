# Trades

Apollo matches people who want to swap objekts and records each trade. It never moves an objekt: each side sends in the COSMO app, and the outbox drain sees the transfers on chain and settles the trade. This file maps the system; the code holds the detail.

## Where the code lives

- `packages/trades`: pure logic shared by the web and schedules apps. `lifecycle.ts` (every trade, offer and listing transition), `allocate.ts` and `assignment.ts` (fitting a claimer's objekts to a listing's wants), `holdings.ts` (why a token can't be offered, and `pickSerials()`), `range.ts` (the range filter schema and `matchesRange()`), `composer.ts` (the listing composer's row helpers), `transfers.ts` (the drain's per-row decisions), `timers.ts` (windows and deadlines), `caps.ts` (claim caps and listing limits), `feedback.ts` (rating eligibility and scoring), `listing.ts` (the input schemas) and `events.ts` (the Redis `trade-events` message).
- `packages/database/src/web/schema.ts` and `types.ts`: `trade_offers` (listings and trades in one table, told apart by `kind`), `trade_offer_items`, `trade_listing_wants`, `trade_listing_want_options`, `trade_messages` (chat and timeline), `trade_feedback` and `user_feedback_stats`. The status lists and the `TradeEvent` timeline union live in `types.ts`; the `TradeEvent` in `@apollo/trades/lifecycle` is a different type, the events `transition()` takes.
- `apps/web/src/lib/functions`: `trade-listings.ts` (post, edit, withdraw, claim, the listing page), `listing-feed.ts`, `trade-offers.ts`, `trades.ts` (the trade page, My trades, cancel, dispute), `feedback.ts`, `reports.ts` and `staff-trades.ts`. Their transaction helpers are in `lib/server/trades/`, plus `lib/server/trades.server.ts` (`liveTradesBlocker()`, `closeOpenOffers()`), `staff-trades.server.ts` (`actOnTrade()`), `feedback.server.ts` (`addFeedbackStats()`) and `trade-events.server.ts` (`publishTradeUpdates()`).
- `apps/schedules`: `drain-outbox` (transfers), `trade-timers` (deadlines and offer expiry) and `chat-retention`. See `apps/schedules/CLAUDE.md`.
- `apps/chat` and `packages/chat`: the trade chat. See `apps/chat/CLAUDE.md`.

Every writer locks users, then trades, then listings; the full order is under Lock order in `docs/auth.md`.

## Listings

A listing offers a pool of specific tokens the poster holds, in units: one unit is `unit_size` of the pool's tokens for the listing's want rows at their counts, so the listing reads as a ratio of `unit_size` to the rows' total. A claim takes some number of units, picking that many units of tokens from the pool and giving each row's count times the units. A listing whose unit is its whole pool is a bundle, everything for everything, which is what a listing without a unit size posts as. The want rows ask for collections: exact rows (one collection, a count), range rows (an array per column, where an empty array matches any value; values in a column are OR'd and columns AND'd) or choice rows (a count of any mix of a named set of collections, stored in `trade_listing_want_options`). The composer posts a range whose members are one artist's whole roster as that artist (`collapseWholeGroup()`); members of several artists stay members, since an artist beside another group's members would match nothing. Wanting a specific serial is left to the listing's note. The limits are in `caps.ts`. Posting copies each offered objekt's collection values, except its collection number, onto its item and unions them into the listing's arrays, so the feed filters without crossing databases (see the comments on `trade_offers_feed_filters_idx`). A range row's collection numbers are matched against the indexer's current value, which staff can edit.

| Status      | Reached by                                                                                 |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `open`      | posting; it stays open while its trades start and end, as long as its pool can make a unit |
| `filled`    | its pool falling short of a unit, with no trade live, once one of its trades completed     |
| `broken`    | its pool falling short of a unit, with no trade live and none completed                    |
| `withdrawn` | the poster, or the poster's account deletion                                               |
| `removed`   | the poster being banned                                                                    |
| `claimed`   | nothing; no listing holds it, and the value stays in `trade_offer_status`                  |
| `timed_out` | nothing; older listings that closed when their one trade timed out hold it                 |

Listings don't expire. The poster can edit an open listing, which replaces its items and wants in one transaction and moves `updated_at`, even while it has live trades: those keep their own copies of the tokens they took, and the edit may keep a token that is live in one of the listing's own trades in its pool (`checkTerms()` with the edited listing), where it is free again if that trade is cancelled. The composer's reads, `$fetchOfferedTokenRefusals` and `$fetchTradeTokenStates`, take the edited listing too, once the server confirms it is the caller's, so neither flags nor greys out what Save would keep. Post and Save send the unit size the composer's ratio holds (below). The drain dropping a token from the pool moves `updated_at` too; claims and completions don't, since the tokens they touch were already out of the free pool. A claim carries the `updated_at` the claimer saw and is refused with `listing_updated` if it changed. A token may sit in several open listings at once; only a live trade reserves it, and a pool token with a live copy can't be picked by another claim.

The composer (`components/trade-listings/listing-composer.tsx`, its form helpers in `lib/universal/trade-listing-form.ts`) edits the posting input directly, and its UI speaks in ratios, never units:

- The Ratio control shows once the want rows ask for something and two or more objekts are offered, or when an edit's ratio takes more than the pool holds. The poster steps their side, the unit size, from 1 to the pool's size, and the other side is the want rows' total, under "Each trade is 1 of the objekts you're offering for 2 of what you're looking for" and how many trades the pool makes.
- The form's unit size is either none, a bundle that takes the whole pool however the pool changes, or a number. A new listing starts as a bundle, and stepping the ratio up to the pool's size makes it one again (`stepUnitSize()`). A number never grows: removing objekts holds it to the smaller pool, but not below 1, and adding them leaves it be (`followUnitSize()`), so a ratio that met the pool stays a ratio when the poster restocks; one held at the pool's size steps once more to become a bundle.
- An edit prefills the pool, the listing's own live objekts included, and the unit size if the listing has a ratio (`hasRatio()`, against everything it has offered), else none (`listingToFormValues()`). The tokens that left the pool aren't carried in. The unit size isn't held to the pool: tokens sent in a live trade leave the pool while that trade keeps the listing open, so the pool can hold less than a trade takes, and then the Ratio control shows and Save waits for the poster to lower the ratio or add objekts.
- An "any of" row is filled through the collection search exact rows use, its collections shown as removable cards, with a count. The first range row carries a note that the listing will take offers, so the poster picks the serials, and points to "any of" for specific collections.
- The footer holds Post and Save back with a translated reason for what the schema refuses (`composerBlock()`), including an "any of" row with fewer than two or more than `MAX_CHOICE_OPTIONS` collections or one collection twice, two rows naming the same set (`choicesDistinct()`) and a unit size over the pool; every row's count goes into the total the steppers keep under `MAX_TOTAL_WANTED`. A collection the indexer doesn't know is refused by the server with `listing_want_unknown`.

A listing closes by `listingClosing()`: once its pool can no longer make a unit and none of its trades is live, it is filled if any of them completed, else broken. Every close that breaks a listing, whether the drain dropped a token or a trade ended, sends `listing_closed` to its poster in the same transaction unless they are banned: it names the objekt when the drain dropped one, and names none when a cancelled or timed-out trade left the listing broken, since no single token did. A fill notifies no one. A trade that completes takes its tokens out of the pool, whether the drain saw them arrive or staff completed it; one that is cancelled or times out leaves the tokens it hadn't sent free in the pool again, and the listing stays open if its pool can still make a unit. A token that leaves the pool, given in a trade or gone from the poster, isn't deleted: its row is marked `left_at` and stays as the listing's history, every read of the pool skips it, and an edit replaces only the tokens still in the pool. The rule is applied after every trade ends (`endTrade()` in both apps) and after the drain drops tokens.

Claiming (`$claimListing`, `lib/server/trades/claim.server.ts`) submits how many units it takes, the pool tokens it picked (units times `unit_size`) and one token list per want row (each row's count times the units). The server refuses through `posterBlocked()` when the poster's account or terms stop any claim, refuses a picked token the poster can't give now with `listing_pick_unavailable` naming it, checks each of the claimer's tokens fits its scaled row, is theirs, transferable, unlocked and not live, and creates an `accepted` trade with a 48-hour deadline (`CLAIM_WINDOW`) holding the picked tokens and the claimer's, all copied as live items. The listing row is locked `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` behind its status and `updated_at` but not changed, so claims on one listing queue behind each other and run side by side; a claimer holds one live trade or pending offer per listing (`listing_claim_exists`). `trade_offer_items_live_token_idx` (`UNIQUE (token_id) WHERE live`) keeps a token in at most one live trade, which also catches a claim racing a trade from another listing for the same token.

A listing with any range row takes offers instead of claims (`takesOffers()` in `lifecycle.ts`), since a range matches many serials and only the poster knows which they meant; one with only exact and "any of" rows is claimed at once. The server decides from the listing's rows as the claim's preflight reads them, which the lock's `updated_at` guard holds to. The same `$claimListing` request, through the same preflight and the same checks under the same locks except the claim cap, then creates a `pending` trade proposed by the claimer (`proposed_by` is `to`) that expires after the offer window, holding the picked tokens (keeping `listing_id`) and the claimer's, none of them live, starts its timeline with `offered` and tells the poster with `offer_received`; the result says it is pending. Nothing is reserved, so offers on one listing may pick the same tokens. The poster accepts or declines it and the claimer may withdraw it, through the offer functions; it can't be countered. Accepting (`$acceptOffer`) reads the indexer for both sides and the listing's want rows and unit size before the transaction, then locks the users, the offer and the listing, still open or else `listing_closed`, and judges it again as a claim (`recheckListingOffer()` in `claim.server.ts`, which reads the facts under the locks and refuses as `offerRefusal()` decides, the verdict the poster's Offers tab shows): the picks are still in the pool and a whole number of the listing's current units, and the claimer's tokens fill the want rows scaled to those units exactly (`listingOfferRefusal()` in `allocate.ts`, which allocates them afresh since the trade doesn't store which row each token was for), else `listing_updated`, though a claimer token the indexer doesn't know is `offer_unavailable` naming it; the poster's link (`cosmo_not_linked`); the picks deliverable (`claim_token_unavailable`, naming the token and why) and in no other live trade (`listing_pick_unavailable`); then the claimer's link to the pinned address, ban and claim cap, refused as `offer_unavailable` without saying why so the poster can't learn any of them, and their tokens, refused as `offer_unavailable` naming the token but not why. It then becomes `accepted` with the claim's deadline, its items go live and the claimer hears `offer_accepted`. Editing the listing, its ratio included, turns its pending offers `stale` in the edit's transaction (a `stale` event with reason `edited`, and `offer_stale` with no token to each claimer), and withdrawing it declines them (`declined` by `listing`, and `offer_declined`). A ban or an account deletion closes the poster's listings and declines the offers they received in its own transaction (`closeOpenOffers()`). A listing that fills or breaks can't lock its offers first, so it leaves them to `trade-timers`, which declines them the same way within a minute, as it does an offer sent after a withdrawal read the listing's offers; accepting one before then is refused with `listing_closed`.

The feed (`$fetchListingFeed`, `lib/server/trades/feed.server.ts`) shows every open listing the filters match, however much of its pool live trades hold; a listing leaves it once it closes. Each row the page returns counts its pool's free objekts, where free means no live copy and no lock by the poster (`freePoolCount()`: at most `MAX_OFFERED` pool rows per listing, each probing `trade_offer_items_live_token_idx` and `address_token_idx`). The count is selected for the page's rows only, never filtered on, so neither the page nor its total (`$fetchListingFeedTotal`) runs it for every open listing. It reads only the web database, so a free token the poster has since moved still counts until the drain drops it. A lock never reaches the drain, so a locked token stays in the pool, blocked, until the poster unlocks it or edits it out.

Each feed row carries its unit size, that free count, which may be 0, and how many objekts the listing has ever offered. A listing has a ratio (`hasRatio()`) when its unit is smaller than everything it has ever offered, the pool and the tokens that left it, so one whose pool is down to a unit's worth keeps its ratio and a one-objekt listing never has one. A row with a ratio shows it (unit size : the want rows' total) in place of the arrow and how many objekts are free; a bundle keeps the arrow. Choice rows show their collections' thumbnails and read "Any of 4 collections". The "Looking for" filter matches a want row holding every narrowed value: an exact row's copied values, a range's columns (empty matches any value), or one of a choice row's collections in `trade_listing_want_options`, so a member of one collection and the season of another don't add up.

The listing page reads one answer, `$fetchTradeListing`, from one read of the listing row: the public `listing` and the session's `viewer`, whose candidate takes its free count from the public pool. Every listing-page query sits under one key prefix, removed on an account change (`removeViewerQueries()`), and links to the page don't preload it, since a candidate's answer reads their holdings. If any part fails, the page shows its error.

- `listing` is public. It returns the unit size and every objekt the listing has offered, the ones that left the pool included and each objekt once (an objekt offered again after it left shows as its latest row), each with where it stands (`poolAvailability()`): `free`, `in_trade` (a live copy, whoever holds it now), `traded` (`left_at` set, with when, shown dimmed) or `blocked` (moved, untransferable or locked). Its `stop` says what stops every claim on an open one right now (`listingStop()`): `blocked`, a fault on the poster's side, when `posterBlocked()` holds or less than a unit is free or in a trade; `busy` when a unit is there but trades hold so much of it that less than a unit is free, which the page shows the poster and everyone else as a note to wait rather than a fault. It never names a claimer. A filled listing names the COSMO account behind its latest completed trade and counts its completed trades.
- `viewer` is the session's role: `anonymous` when signed out, and on a closed listing `closed`, with the latest trade from it the viewer was a party to. On an open one:
  - `poster`: the pending offers, each with its claimer, both sides' tokens with serials, its expiry and whether it can be accepted now (`offerRefusal()`, in `$acceptOffer`'s order and with its error codes, reading the indexer once for the page, the claimers' caps in one grouped statement and the locks by address), and every trade the listing started with when it was accepted and the pool tokens it took, a live one with what the poster still has to send and whether it waits on them, and a disputed one with the dispute that froze it, all from one query (`readListingTrades()`).
  - `claimer` or `offerer`: a viewer with a live trade or a pending offer from the listing, which a claimer holds at most one of; an offerer's comes with both sides' tokens, so the page can say when another trade took one of its picks.
  - `candidate`: anyone else, with the most units they can take (`allocateUnits()`, bounded by the free pool, the caps and their holdings), per want row how many matching objekts they own and how many it is short, and how their latest offer on the listing ended when the poster or the listing ended it (`endedOffer()`, from the one read of their latest trade on it that also finds a live trade or pending offer: declined, expired or stale), so the page can say so over the picker. Every want row starts empty and the viewer fills it from their collection; stepping the units down trims each row's picks to its new count. A refetch keeps both sides' picks unless the terms or the most units changed, dropping a pool pick that's no longer free (`pickerVersion()`). Only a candidate's read touches holdings, and the claim cap only stops a listing that claims at once.

The poster's own open listing has Listing, Offers and Trades tabs, the open one in the `tab` search param (`listingSearchSchema`, left out of the URL for Listing), and the header keeps Edit and Withdraw on all of them and says the listing is theirs, when they posted it and when it last changed (`components/trade-listings/poster-panels.tsx`):

- Listing shows both sides. Each objekt says how it stands and who has it (`poolStanding()`): free, in a live trade from the listing (naming its claimer) or from elsewhere, sent (already sent in a live trade from the listing, which the drain marks as leaving the pool, naming its claimer), traded (naming who it went to), gone (it left the pool in neither, so it left the poster's account) or blocked with its reason. A token that left the pool went in the newest trade holding it that was accepted by then and hadn't ended before, so one traded, offered again in an edit and then moved is gone. The want rows say how many of the poster's they are for on a listing with a ratio.
- Offers shows while the listing takes offers or one is still pending. Each offer names its claimer and their rating, its ratio, both sides with serials and its expiry. Accept and Decline go through the trade page's offer dialog, and an offer `offerRefusal()` refuses says why in `$acceptOffer`'s words, told to the poster (`formatOfferRefusal()`), naming the claimer of the listing's own trade that holds a pick, and can only be declined.
- Trades lists the live trades, each saying where it stands as My trades does (what the poster still has to send, that the claimer sends, or a dispute open or paused), then the finished ones, each opening its trade page.

Offers counts the pending offers and Trades the live trades (`posterTabCounts()`); a tab the listing no longer has falls back to Listing (`shownPosterTab()`).

## Trades

A trade's `from` side is the poster or a direct offer's sender, and `to` the claimer or the offer's recipient. A trade that starts as an offer records the side that proposed it in `proposed_by` (`from` for a direct offer or a counter, `to`, the claimer, for an offer on a listing), which it keeps once the offer ends: the proposer may withdraw it and only the other side answers it. A claim starts accepted and leaves it null, as listings do (`trade_offers_proposed_chk`). `transition()` in `lifecycle.ts` is the lifecycle:

- `accepted`: a claim or an accepted offer. Each side sends its tokens in COSMO to the other's pinned address before `deadline_at`.
- `completed`: every item arrived, or staff completed it.
- `cancelled`: a party cancelled while nothing had been sent (`cancelled_by` names who), or staff cancelled it. A party can't cancel once the indexer shows any token at its receiver, even before the drain records it; after that a dispute is the only way out.
- `timed_out`: the deadline passed with nothing sent. Its tokens go back to the listing's pool.
- `disputed`: a party opened a dispute, a ban froze the trade, or the deadline passed with something sent and something still owed. The deadline stops, transfers still record, and only staff end it.

Parties are matched by user id and pinned addresses (`from_address`, `to_address`) are fixed when the claim's trade or the offer is created, so relinking COSMO doesn't redirect a trade. A live trade (`accepted` or `disputed`) blocks either party's account deletion (`liveTradesBlocker()`).

Each change writes a typed event to `trade_messages` and the in-app notifications in the same transaction (`insertTradeEvent()` and `notifyUsers()` in `lib/server/trades/trade.server.ts`), then publishes the trade's id on Redis `trade-events` after commit (`publishTradeUpdates()`), which apps/chat turns into live updates on open trade pages.

## Direct offers and counters

Trade Matches on a have/want list pair lets a user pick tokens on both sides and send an offer (`$sendOffer`). The offer is a `pending` trade that reserves nothing, proposed by its sender, its `from` side. The side that didn't propose it accepts it (it becomes `accepted` with the claim's 48-hour deadline and its tokens go live), declines it, or counters it; its proposer can withdraw it. The pending `TradeState` carries the side that proposed it: `from` for a direct offer or counter, and `to` for an offer on a listing, whose poster's items came from the listing (`tradeListingId()`); `tradeState()` throws on a pending row that is neither. `transition()` refuses a counter to an offer on a listing with `listing_offer`, which the web app answers as `offer_unavailable`, and `$counterOffer` refuses one before any indexer work. A counter is a new pending offer from the side that countered, again its `from` side, with `parent_id` pointing at the original, which ends as `countered`, and it starts its own window. The timeline's `declined` and `withdrawn` events name the side that acted, and every notice about an offer (accepted, declined, countered, expired, stale) goes to its proposer.

A pending offer expires 72 hours after it was sent (`OFFER_WINDOW`, `expires_at`), judged by the database's `now()` rather than the chain watermark, because nothing on chain decides when an offer ends. When the drain sees one of its tokens leave its holder or turn untransferable, the offer turns `stale` and its proposer is told. A token that goes live in another trade leaves the offer pending, and accepting it is then refused. Offers don't count toward the claim cap and have no cap of their own; an offer on a listing counts once it's accepted, which checks the claimer's cap. Chat opens only once an offer is accepted.

## Completion: the outbox drain

The indexer appends a `list_event_outbox` row for every transfer and transferability change, and `drain-outbox` in apps/schedules applies each batch to the web database in one transaction (`applyTrades`). `foldTransfers()` and `decideTransfer()` in `packages/trades/src/transfers.ts` decide each row in order:

- A pinned token reaching its receiver's pinned address marks the item sent, with the chain time and hash, whichever wallet it came from, so a sender who routes it through another of their wallets still delivers. A trade with nothing left owed completes and takes its tokens out of its listing's pool; a disputed trade records the transfer but waits for staff.
- A token leaving elsewhere, or turning untransferable, posts a `token_moved` or `token_untransferable` event on its live trade and turns pending offers holding it stale (`breakOffers`).
- A token leaving its poster for anywhere, its own trade's claimer included, or turning untransferable, is dropped from the pool of every open listing offering it, which then closes by `listingClosing()` (`breakOffers`).
- A row whose indexer `transfer` row no longer exists (a hot-block rollback) is skipped for trades.

Only the exact token ids pinned on the trade count; a party who sends a different copy sorts it out in chat or through a dispute. Marking an item sent is idempotent, so a replayed row can't advance a trade twice.

## Timers

`trade-timers` runs every minute. It ends accepted trades whose `deadline_at` is at or before the drain's watermark, `list_drain_cursor.chain_time`, never `now()`: a transfer stamped before the deadline but drained late still counts, because the watermark only reaches the deadline once that transfer is drained (`isTimedOut()`). A due trade nobody owes anything on completes. One where something was sent and something is still owed is disputed under a `deadline_passed` report (`disputeAtDeadline()`), since a side may be out of pocket. The report names as its reporter the side that sent everything it owed (`waitingSide()`), or no one when both sent part: staff settle it as any dispute, and the parties read it as a pause for review. Otherwise, with nothing sent, it times out and its tokens are free in its listing's pool again. It logs an error while there is no watermark or it trails the clock by more than 10 minutes, since timeouts trail the watermark. Pending offers expire in a separate transaction on the clock, and another declines the pending offers on listings that are no longer open (see `apps/schedules/CLAUDE.md`). Staff extend a disputed trade with `extendedDeadline()`: the time left when it froze plus 24, 48 or 72 hours. An extend is refused with `trade_party_banned` while either party is banned, since they couldn't sign in to finish it; staff complete or cancel it instead.

## Claim caps and feedback

A user may hold `claimCap()` claims at once (accepted or disputed trades they claimed from a listing, counted by `claimStanding()`): 3, plus one per score tier reached in `CLAIM_CAP_TIERS`, up to `CLAIM_CAP_CEILING`. Open listings and pending offers aren't capped; accepting an offer on a listing counts toward the claimer's cap, and one refused for it reads to the poster as the generic `offer_unavailable`, since nobody but the claimer may learn their cap.

The parties of a completed or timed-out trade, or one staff cancelled, can rate each other for 14 days after it ends, while its chat can still be read (`feedbackOpening()`, `FEEDBACK_WINDOW_DAYS`); a party-cancelled trade can't be rated, and a disputed one waits for staff. A rating shows at once, can't be edited, and notifies the rated party. It counts toward the score only when the trade completed or a token arrived (`feedbackScored()`), so a timed-out trade stays on record without being farmable. `user_feedback_stats` is keyed by COSMO address and updated by one atomic upsert in the rating's transaction (`addFeedbackStats()`); the score is positives minus negatives. Staff remove ratings with `feedback: ["remove-any"]`, which takes a scored rating back out of the stats. Ratings show on `/@{username}/feedback`, with badges on listings and trades.

## Chat

Each trade has a chat in apps/chat, a separate Effect service the browser reaches over a websocket (`VITE_CHAT_URL`), authenticated with short-lived Better Auth JWTs (see Chat tokens in `docs/auth.md`). The web app never reads or writes message text. Bodies are AES-256-GCM ciphertext under `MESSAGE_KEY`, bound to the trade id. Access is decided only in `packages/chat/src/policy.ts`: parties join a trade from `accepted` onward and send only while it is live; staff with `trade: ["read-any"]` join only while the trade or one of its messages has an open report or dispute. Under that report, staff with `trade: ["message"]` also write to both parties, on live and ended trades alike; other staff read only. A staff message is stored with `trade_messages.by_staff`, notifies both parties with `trade_message` naming the staff member, and is audited as `trade:message`. Every viewer, its author included, sees it on the left in an orange bubble under the staff member's COSMO username (else their Apollo username) and a staff label; once their account is deleted it reads as "{appName} staff". Parties can't report a staff message. Removing a message needs `trade: ["remove-message"]` and is audited. `chat-retention` blanks message bodies 14 days after the trade ends (`CHAT_RETENTION_DAYS`); events stay, and a reported message keeps its own encrypted copy in `report_messages`.

## Disputes and staff actions

A party opens a dispute (`$openDispute`) on an accepted trade before its deadline passes the watermark. It files a `trade` report, sets `dispute_report_id` and freezes the trade. Staff act through `lib/functions/staff-trades.ts`: with `trade: ["cancel-any"]` they cancel any accepted or disputed trade from its trade page, with a required note (the poster's tokens go back to the listing's pool), and with `trade: ["resolve-dispute"]` they complete or extend a disputed one from `/admin/reports`, which can also cancel it. Each action names the dispute report the trade is under (none for an undisputed trade), is refused if that has changed, and resolves the trade's open reports in the same transaction. Staff can't act on or see reports about trades they are a party to. A ban disputes the user's accepted trades under a `party_banned` report, removes their open listings and closes their pending offers (`banAndSweep()`, see Bans in `docs/auth.md`). Staff read any trade at `/trade/$id` with `trade: ["read-any"]`.

`listing: ["remove-any"]` is in the permission statement, but no server function uses it and `listing` report targets aren't supported, so banning the poster is the only way staff remove a listing.

## Launch gate

The trade system is gated by `market: ["access"]` (`marketAccess` in `@apollo/auth`), held by testers, moderators and admins; signed-out visitors count as `user`. A tester holds market access and nothing else, so people trying the market before launch see it as a plain user will rather than as staff. The gate covers pages and entry points only. `/market/*` (through `routes/market/route.tsx`), `/trade/$id` and `/@{$username}/feedback` call `requireMarketAccess()` in `beforeLoad` and 404 without it. The navbar's Market links, the My trades links in the user menu and notification bell, the report button on profiles and the offer picking in Trade Matches check `useHasPermission()(marketAccess)`. The server functions aren't gated, with one exception: a direct offer only goes to someone who holds market access, since without it they couldn't open it to answer. Trade Matches still lists them, marked not `offerable`, and `preflightOffer()` refuses a send or counter to them with `offer_unavailable`.

Launching is adding `market: statement.market` to the `user` role in `packages/auth/src/index.ts` and setting `user: true` on the `market` row of `packages/auth/tests/roles.test.ts`, then deploying; there is no database step. Removing it again works as a kill switch only while no one outside staff has a live trade: after that, plain users can no longer open their trades or their chat while the deadlines keep running, so their trades time out, or are disputed where something was sent. Past that point, staff cancel the live trades from their trade pages instead (`trade: ["cancel-any"]`).

## Market notice

`/market`, `/market/collections` and listing pages show an alert dialog until the visitor accepts it (`components/market/market-notice.tsx`). Accept calls `$acceptMarketNotice`, which sets the httpOnly `market-notice` cookie to `MARKET_NOTICE_VERSION` for a year (`lib/functions/market-notice.ts`). The `/market` route reads the cookie on the server, so SSR renders the dialog open or closed with no flash; signed-out visitors see it too. Bumping `MARKET_NOTICE_VERSION` shows a changed notice to everyone again. Its claim-cap copy reads `CLAIM_CAP_BASE`, `CLAIM_CAP_TIERS` and `CLAIM_CAP_CEILING`, so it can't drift from `claimCap()`.

## Token ids across the databases

The web database stores token ids as `integer` (`trade_offer_items.token_id`, like pins and binder entries), and the schemas cap them at 2^31 − 1 (`tokenIdSchema` in `listing.ts`). The indexer stores them as text: `objekt.id` is `varchar` and `list_event_outbox.token_id` is `text`. Convert at the boundary: `String(tokenId)` into an indexer query, `Number(row.id)` out of one (`readIndexerTokens()` in `lib/server/trades/holdings.server.ts`, and the drain in `apps/schedules`).

## Unused payment schema

The schema carries a payment side that nothing creates or acts on. It is the starting point for sales:

- Enums `payment_method` (`paymentMethods`) and `listing_offer_mode` (`listingOfferModes`) in `packages/database/src/web/types.ts`.
- The `payment` kind on `trade_item_kind` and `trade_want_kind`, with `trade_offer_items.amount`, `currency`, `method`, `marked_paid_at` and `confirmed_at`, `trade_listing_wants.amount` and `currency`, their branches of `trade_offer_items_objekt_chk` and `trade_listing_wants_shape_chk`, and `trade_listing_wants_payment_idx` (one payment row per listing).
- `trade_offers.offers` (the offers mode, listings only; `trade_offers_trade_chk` keeps it null on trades).
- The `marked_paid` and `payment_confirmed` events in `TradeEvent` and `packages/chat/src/items.ts`, which `trade-events.tsx` renders as nothing.
- In `packages/trades`: the payment `TradeItemState`, the `mark_paid` and `confirm_payment` events in `transition()`, and payments in `owingSides()`, `feedbackScored()` and `feedbackRole()`.
- The listing feed's want matching (`lookingForConditions()` in `apps/web/src/lib/server/trades/feed.server.ts`), which never matches the payment row.
- The `payment_not_received` dispute reason, which `open-dispute-dialog.tsx` offers only on a trade with a payment.

What keeps it unreachable: `listingInputSchema` and `editListingInputSchema` take only exact, range and choice wants and token ids in strict objects, the offer schemas take token-id arrays, every insert writes `kind: "objekt"`, `posterBlocked()` refuses a claim on a listing with a payment want, `listingTypes` is `["trade"]`, and no server function runs `mark_paid` or `confirm_payment`. Adding sales means lifting those, plus a new `user.payment_methods` column, a buyer/seller notification or two, an FX snapshot on the trade, and moving market price data from sale lists to sale listings (`sync-market-stats`).
